# The competition, from where I sit

Three producers were given the same repo and the same brief. What each of us chose
is, in the end, the entry: the topic is the pitch.

| producer | film | the idea underneath it |
|---|---|---|
| **eidolon** (me) | **THE SIGNAL AT 37.5** — the 1977 Wow! signal | *a receipt that outlived every explanation* |
| chronoscope | THIRTY BRONZE WHEELS — the Antikythera Mechanism | *a machine that computed the sky* |
| thaumatrope | THE THIRTEEN-HOUR AVALANCHE — the 1929 cable breaks | *an accident that became an instrument* |

They are three different films and, pleasingly, three different arguments about
evidence: a measurement nobody can repeat, a mechanism nobody expected, and a
disaster that turned out to be a measurement. Nobody overlapped, and nobody had to
be told to stay off anyone else's folder.

## What I took from the other two (in the open)

- **From thaumatrope:** a machine-readable `delivery.json` next to the film, and a
  `claims/sources` table that a reviewer can check line by line. I copied the shape
  of that idea (`docs/QC.md` + `FILM.md` §7) because it makes the film auditable
  without watching me work.
- **From chronoscope:** the treatment-first discipline. Their `FILM.md` states the
  audience promise in one sentence before any production exists; mine now does too.
- **Not taken:** generated photo plates. Their film uses them well, but for this
  subject a single hand-drawn visual language (ink, paper, amber, cyan) is the
  stronger choice — a 1977 computer printout should look like paper, not a render.

## What I did not do to their work

Read only. No edits, no deletions, no reverts, no force-pushes, no "improvements"
to their folders, and no rebase that would erase their commits — when the remote
moved ahead of me I merged it. The competition is the film, not the history.
