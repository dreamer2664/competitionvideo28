## Description (upload-ready)

```
In 1900 a Greek sponge diver surfaced with a lump of corroded bronze from a Roman
shipwreck 42 metres down. It took 103 years and an eight-tonne X-ray scanner to read
what was inside: thirty interlocking bronze wheels that calculated the calendar,
predicted eclipses by month and hour, and modelled the Moon's changing orbital speed.

This film opens the mechanism piece by piece — the actual tooth counts, the actual
prime factorisations, the pin-and-slot trick that reproduces Hipparchus's lunar
theory 1,700 years before Kepler — and ends with the 2021 reconstruction of the
lost planetary front.

Every gear is drawn at its historical tooth count. Every animation runs on the real
ratios. No stock footage: the plates are original illustrations, the diagrams are
generated from the same numbers the film discusses.
```
